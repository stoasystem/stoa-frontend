# Home V2 v6.5 Final Photography Art Direction

**Date:** 2026-07-06
**Style Target:** Swiss private-school / high-end education service 70%, modern learning product 30%

## Visual Principles

- Quiet confidence over performance.
- Natural family learning over staged tutoring.
- Light, space, and restraint over feature display.
- Child agency stays visible.
- Parents are close, informed, and calm, not supervising anxiously.
- Technology can appear, but it should not dominate the story.

## Hero Image Direction

### Story

A real question has appeared during study. The student is still doing the work. A parent is nearby in a calm, supportive way. The image should feel like the beginning of clarity, not a finished success pose.

### Composition

- Primary composition must work as a tall editorial crop.
- Secondary composition must work as a mobile landscape crop.
- Keep a soft negative-space zone for the proof panel.
- Prefer side, three-quarter, or partial-face angles over direct camera smiles.
- The human gesture matters more than the laptop.

### Light And Color

- Natural window light.
- Warm whites, quiet wood, grey, cream, soft burgundy or muted green accents.
- Avoid saturated orange/yellow/red interiors.
- Avoid cool corporate blue.
- Avoid busy shelves, toys, posters, and classroom signage.

### People

- Swiss/European plausibility without cliché.
- Parent and child should feel like a real family moment, not hired models smiling at camera.
- Child is not distressed or helpless.
- Parent is not correcting aggressively.

## Parent Confidence Image Direction

### Story

Parents can see learning progress more clearly without taking over the work. The image should suggest a calm weekly check-in, a notebook review, or a shared view of learning rhythm.

### Composition

- Parent and child can be in frame, but the child's work remains central.
- Partial-face, hands, notebook, over-table, or side-angle compositions are welcome.
- The weekly-note overlay needs clean space.
- Avoid parent-over-shoulder surveillance.

### Emotional Tone

- Transparent.
- Warm.
- Mature.
- Calm enough for a Swiss parent audience.
- No dashboard-like or productivity-software feeling.

## Rejection Rules

Reject any candidate or shot that feels:

- Like generic tutoring stock.
- Like a school endorsement.
- Like the parent is monitoring or controlling the child.
- Like an AI/tech product hero.
- Too colorful, toy-like, or social-media staged.
- Too luxurious in a lifestyle-ad sense.
- Too gloomy or problem-focused.
- Dependent on front-facing smiles.
