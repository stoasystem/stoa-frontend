## Observed German Headline Style

The company homepage favors short, editorial German headline rhythm over literal explanatory sentences. Large headings work as compact phrases, often with sentence fragments or paired concepts. The style is calm and confident rather than loud or sales-driven.

Observed pattern:

- short headline units
- clear rhythm
- little technical wording
- explanatory detail moved into body copy
- premium education tone instead of SaaS phrasing

Learning-platform implication:

- keep German hero and section titles short
- use `titleLines` or stacked title rhythm for large display headings
- avoid long German compounds in hero typography
- explain complete meaning in subtitles and supporting copy

## Observed German CTA Style

German CTAs on the company homepage are concise and conventional. They read as service-oriented actions, not aggressive conversion copy.

Useful CTA style signals:

- `Mehr erfahren`
- `Jetzt anmelden`
- `Kontakt aufnehmen`
- `Anmeldeformular`

Learning-platform implication:

- prefer direct actions such as `Lernen starten`, `Frage stellen`, `Bericht ansehen`, `Plan wählen`, `Weiter`
- avoid long labels such as `Professionelle Lehrpersonenunterstützung anfragen`
- keep mobile CTA labels short enough for one line where possible

## Observed Sentence Length

The homepage allows fuller German explanation in paragraphs, but keeps visual anchors short. Long clauses are not used as the main display headline.

Learning-platform implication:

- use short titles and slightly fuller subtitles
- split long German UI copy into two sentences when readability improves
- avoid placing long explanatory text inside buttons, badges, card titles, or nav items

## Observed Tone

The German brand voice is:

- formal enough for parents and institutions
- calm
- precise
- education-centered
- premium without sounding distant

The tone avoids hype, technical terms, and exaggerated promises.

## Observed Education Wording

Useful vocabulary signals:

- `Schülerinnen und Schüler`
- `Lehrpersonen`
- `Verständnis`
- `Sicherheit`
- `Selbstvertrauen`
- `Lernfortschritt`
- `Begleitung`
- `Förderung`
- `Lernumgebung`

`Nachhilfe` is natural German, but it can feel like a traditional tutoring category. For STOA brand moments, prefer `Lernunterstützung`, `Begleitung`, `Förderung`, or direct learning language.

## What To Adopt

- Short, composed German headings.
- CTA labels that sound like normal product actions.
- Formal, trustworthy education wording for parents, teachers, and platform-level surfaces.
- Warmer, direct wording for student chat and learning actions.
- Paragraph copy that explains the service without forcing every concept into the title.

## What Not To Copy

- Do not copy homepage components, layout structures, assets, or full text blocks.
- Do not force the learning platform to sound like a marketing page.
- Do not make chat, dashboard, billing, or report surfaces overly editorial.

## Adaptation Rules For The Learning Platform

1. German display headings should be short phrases, not translated English clauses.
2. Use `Lehrperson` / `Lehrpersonen` for professional support.
3. Use `Lernende`, `Schülerinnen und Schüler`, or role-specific labels based on context.
4. Use `Schülerinnen und Schüler` in parent/institution copy when space allows; use shorter labels in dense UI.
5. Keep CTAs short and action-oriented.
6. Place complex meaning in subtitles, descriptions, or help text.
7. Adjust layout when copy remains naturally longer after localization.
